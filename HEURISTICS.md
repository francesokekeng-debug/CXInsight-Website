# How the site applies Nielsen's 10 usability heuristics

Heuristics are rules of thumb for spotting problems, not a pass/fail score. Treat this as a design record, then test the site with 3–5 real visitors to find what it misses.

| # | Heuristic | Where it shows up on the site |
|---|-----------|-------------------------------|
| 1 | Visibility of system status | The current page is highlighted in the menu; breadcrumbs on every inner page; "In progress / Published" labels on every piece; a reading-progress bar on articles; the filter says how many pieces it is showing. |
| 2 | Match between system and the real world | Plain-English labels named after what customers actually do (Cancelling, Complaining, Switching, Moving home); each journey introduced as the question a customer would ask. |
| 3 | User control and freedom | "Skip to content" link; the mobile menu closes with its button or the Escape key; "Back to top" in the footer; any filter can be reset with "All" or "Show all". |
| 4 | Consistency and standards | One set of colours, type and components everywhere; the same section names in the header, footer and breadcrumbs; links always underlined in ochre; one ochre button per view. |
| 5 | Error prevention | No forms to get wrong; the email is copied with a button that confirms it worked, instead of a mail link that can silently fail; draft work is clearly labelled so nobody quotes it as final. |
| 6 | Recognition rather than recall | Navigation always visible (sticky header); "Start where it's useful to you" shows routes for recruiters, researchers and founders; CV always one click away; "On this page" contents list in articles; "Where next?" links at the end. |
| 7 | Flexibility and efficiency of use | Filter by journey, with the filter saved in the link (e.g. investigations/#cancelling) so it can be bookmarked or shared; keyboard support throughout; print-friendly articles. |
| 8 | Aesthetic and minimalist design | One signature element (the hand-drawn line, drawn once on load); lots of white space; colour is rare: indigo for text and the line, ochre for the fix and the main action, earth red only where journeys break. |
| 9 | Help users recognise, diagnose and recover from errors | The "page not found" page explains what may have happened and offers three ways forward; empty filters say what's missing and offer one click back to everything. |
| 10 | Help and documentation | "About this site" explains investigations, the status labels and field notes. |

## Accessibility basics included
- Atkinson Hyperlegible body font (designed by the Braille Institute for legibility)
- Visible keyboard focus on every interactive element
- Touch targets at least 44px high
- Motion switched off for visitors who ask their device to reduce it
- Text colours chosen to meet WCAG AA contrast on their backgrounds
